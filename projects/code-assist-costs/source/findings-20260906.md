# Findings: affordable code-assist configurations

## Decision and limitations

Optimize spend per accepted correct task and expected business loss, not input
tokens alone. The simulation compares configurations that a harness may or may
not expose. It does not establish that Pi, Copilot or another product is an
empirical winner. Prices are documented; all quality probabilities, context-rot
curves, task sizes, losses, cache realization and monthly disturbances are
explicit assumptions.

The official [Copilot rate cards](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/reference/copilot-billing/models-and-pricing)
put Luna's long-input tier above 200k, while its
[direct API card](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-luna)
puts it above 272k. This is a route variable, not a universal model property.
Terra and Sol use 272k in the selected Copilot cards. All amounts below are USD
token usage, before any subscription, included allowance or tax.

## High-leverage numerical results

For 1000 additional stable system tokens across ten requests per task, with one
write and nine cache reads and no other change:

| Selected model | Extra per 1000 tasks | Extra per million tasks |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| GPT-5.6 Luna | $0.43 | $430 |
| GPT-5.6 Terra | $4.30 | $4300 |
| GPT-5.6 Sol | $8.60 | $8600 |
| Claude Sonnet 4.6 | $6.45 | $6450 |

For Luna the same tokens cost $2000 per million tasks if ordinary uncached
input is paid on every request, or $2500 if every request writes cache with no
subsequent hit. This is a marginal prefix increment inside a cache-eligible
prompt, not a claim that a standalone 1000-token prompt is cacheable.

In the short workload, reducing five sequential tool operations to four saves
$1.386 per 1000 tasks with Luna. Keeping all five but batching independent calls
in waves of at most two saves $1.016. In the ten-tool feature workload, batching
all calls in pairs saves $6.20 per 1000 tasks, while dropping one saves $3.832.
The long workload is dependent and receives no batching saving.

Dropping a required tool is not free semantically. In the assumed smooth world,
short-task correct completion falls from 96.03% to 94.20%. With equal loss for
each non-correctly-completed task, the saving disappears once such a failure
costs about $0.0756. This is a calculated break-even under the assumed penalty,
not a measured tool's value.

Changing an early prefix on every request increases Luna's modeled spend by
225.7% for short tasks, 314.1% for features and 644.2% for long tasks. Loading a
1k instead of 10k tool-schema prefix saves 24.3%, 9.4% and 2.2%, respectively,
when needed capabilities remain available. Stable identity and selective
loading can matter more than trimming a small always-cached system prompt.

## Compaction is not a universal 200k rule

The long baseline has 38 sequential tool operations, 39 main calls and growth
from 49k to 809k input tokens. Summary input/output and cache rebuild are charged.

| Model | No compaction | 100k cap | 180k cap | 200k cap | 200k with compactor cache reuse |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Luna | $1.10967 | $0.77937 | $0.62651 | $0.78499 | $0.50935 |
| Terra | $10.67350 | $7.79370 | $6.26510 | $5.97990 | $4.54650 |
| Sol | $20.92700 | $14.79540 | $12.01820 | $11.48780 | $8.62100 |

Luna's 180k cap avoids long-tier compactor calls in this trace; the 200k cap
creates four compactor calls above the billing threshold. Frequent 100k
compaction incurs twelve summaries. Neither the smallest cap nor the exact
billing threshold is automatically cheapest. The reuse column is conditional on
support for cache-aware compactor reads and retaining the static prefix.

Cache timing can reverse provider cost ordering. With no scheduled idle gap,
the selected Sonnet long trace costs $8.98035, less than Terra's $10.67350. With
600-second pauses every fifth request and an assumed realized 300-second Sonnet
TTL, it costs $19.34070 while Terra remains $10.67350. This is a TTL sensitivity,
not proof of actual Copilot eviction or provider performance.

## Quality and preferred policies

The lowest spend-per-correct configurations satisfying the illustrative 95%
completion floor in the smooth world cost $5.98, $74.80 and $4320.93 per 1000
short, feature and long tasks. They achieve assumed completion of 97.51%, 95.18%
and 95.96%, respectively. The short optimum drops a tool; it is less attractive
under the business-loss objective and the stronger quality challenge.

More defensible starting configurations within this finite study minimize
worst-world expected-loss regret and retain required tool operations:

| Workload | Configuration | Smooth-world token spend / 1000 | Assumed completion |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Short | Luna, 1k schemas, pair independent tools, keep full outputs, validate and escalate to Terra if rejected | $12.55 | 99.35% |
| Feature | Terra, 1k schemas, pair independent tools, keep full outputs, validate and escalate to Sol if rejected | $386.36 | 98.17% |
| Long | Terra, 1k schemas, retain half tool-result tokens, 180k cap, full model output, escalate to Sol if rejected | $4969.44 | 96.31% |

All use at most two attempts and avoid writing the final never-reused suffix.
The selected 100k caps for short/feature configurations are inactive; their
lexicographic tie-break does not show that 100k is the optimal short-task cap.
The short and feature policies meet the quality floor in all three declared
worlds; the long policy does not. No tested long policy meets 95% in the
critical-cliff world. Low cost is not a valid reason to silently lower that
quality requirement. The robust objective is conditional on the invented error
and recovery losses, and minimax regret is not a probability of optimality.

Published context studies motivate stress testing but do not give a universal
failure threshold for these current coding models. The 100k onset is an
assumption. A particularly strong counterexample is a 50%-fidelity critical-fact
summary stress: Sol's 200k policy costs less per attempt but correct completion
drops to 0.265%, making its cost per correct completion about $4343 versus $98
without compaction. This intentionally severe mathematical scenario illustrates
the danger of judging summary strategies by token spend alone; it is not a
plausibility estimate.

## Monthly affordability and uncertainty

For a 60/30/10 short/feature/long task mixture, 1000 tasks with baseline Luna
have exact expected token usage $131.65 and a simulated p05–p95 of $115.05–$148.53.
The fixed 200k policy gives $99.18 and $87.31–$111.29, respectively, but does not
meet a 95% quality floor under the assumed Luna quality function.

Adding a blind retry/escalation policy to unbounded context is not a cheap fix.
Under the invented shared month-level difficulty shocks, Luna-to-Terra costs
$894.65 per 1000 tasks on average, or $894645 at a million. At the latter volume
the p05–p95 is $867445–$988458; the risk of exceeding a budget 5% above a normal
month's mean is 17.17%. Large volume diversifies independent task noise but not
the shared difficulty shock. The simulation estimates this invented process,
not production uncertainty.

Current [Copilot individual billing](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/billing/usage-based-billing-for-individuals)
implies pairwise least-invoice switches at $44 and $131 of token usage across
Pro, Pro+ and Max, with current variable flex credits. Inside an unused allowance
the marginal cash invoice can be zero even though token usage rises. Above all
allowances, the slope returns to the usage cost. At high usage these three plans
give offsets of $5, $31 and $100 relative to token usage, respectively; none
eliminates the scaling cost. Quotas and million-task feasibility are unresolved.

## Evidence and exploration

The final mathematical bundle is `../artifacts/data/20260906-v3/`, with:

- Core run tables, 24 generated contrasts and an independent skill audit.
- 51840 grid cells, 5760 distinct policies, 4328 request-ledger rows and 320000
  synthetic months in the independently validated analysis extension.
- SQLite, thirteen portable CSV tables, SQL queries and data dictionaries.
- Preflight variables, 49 activation witnesses, and post-run omission review.

The skill's variable-review requirement materially changed the framing: cache
route, compactor overshoot, imperfect validation, correlated retries, nonlinear
subscription billing and lost critical facts were treated as explicit mechanisms
or limits. Passing arithmetic and integrity checks does not resolve the ten
decision-critical omitted or unresolved variable groups. Unrecognized factors
may remain.
