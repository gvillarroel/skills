## Findings and implementation

The original PDF was not changed. The old overlay drew a closed rectangle only
five composition pixels outside each target. That stroke could cross nearby
diagram connectors. Per-cue camera entry/exit took about 0.65 seconds each and
repeatedly restored the overview, exposing clipped neighboring source objects.
The prior audit checked guide clearance but did not check emphasis ink or camera
scale reversals. The user correctly identified a distracting reading experience.

The new pure `paper-motion.js` planner enters gradually once and keeps a common
scale through subsequent pans. Defaults are a maximum 1.3 scale, 2.6-second
entry, two-second preferred pan, 1.4-second minimum pan, 1.1-second reading hold
and 540 composition pixels/second maximum pan speed. Tiny moves are skipped.
When dense cues leave insufficient time, the scene retains its overview rather
than accelerating. These are production defaults, not a medical comfort claim.

The guide travels with a planned pan and arrives at the original speech cue.
Narration, cue times, values and scene budgets are unchanged. A whole active
region and guide inform the common zoom limit. Intersected PDF words expand
target/view bounds so they are not clipped halfway through a word.

Short margin segments replace all closed highlight rectangles. Each candidate
is checked against PDF words, annotated regions and the actual captured raster
pixels, including non-extractable diagram lettering and connectors. Unsafe marks
are omitted; the adjacent primary-red guide remains. This pixel test is not OCR
and does not replace manual inspection of an authored raster crop.

The SEAL limitations now show both page-9 paragraphs in one stable view. Their
separate speech cues move the guide between paragraphs without another crop cut.
The paper's original colors, equations, tables and attribution remain intact.
