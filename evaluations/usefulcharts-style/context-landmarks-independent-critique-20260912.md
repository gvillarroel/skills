# Independent visual review: genealogy landmarks v21

Reviewed on 2026-09-12. This is an unblinded visual review of the current v21 PNG, the v20 PNG, the v19 PNG, and the official royal-family reference. Whole pages were compared at 900 px width. Details were inspected at a common 1200 px page width using browser screenshots of the v21 SVG and the reference image. No raster artwork was edited. The v20 PNG is the before/after comparison baseline. This review does not use record counts, geometry audits, or similarity percentages.

## Verdict

**Replace v19 with v21 as the stronger development candidate. Visual parity with the reference is not established.** The added landmarks now provide useful intermediate reading points without the incorrect local associations visible in v20. This is a substantive improvement in reading the page, although a modest change to its overall composition.

## Regressions and remaining differences

I see no new clipping, text overlap, or obvious connector obstruction in the inspected landmark areas. The main trade-off is the tight pairing of **Everen Isles** with **Everen of Dunport**: the two labels form a small competing heading group. It is a minor hierarchy issue, not a reason to retain v19. Corven's emblem and caption still form a fairly wide horizontal unit, so the caption does more work than the emblem to establish ownership.

The three strongest remaining distinctions from the reference are:

1. **Realm landmarks are still weak optical units.** Their small shields sit to the left of similarly sized italic captions. The reference more often combines an emblem, a short qualifier, and an italic territorial name into a compact, emphatic group. Tighten the emblem–caption spacing or stack them selectively, and give realm and house headings visibly different roles. Use qualifiers only when supported by the source.
2. **Significant figures have limited visual separation from the surrounding records.** Most of v21 uses the same slim nameplate, small reign line, and adjacent spouse treatment. The reference's prominent portraits, larger multiline nameplates, and short explanatory notes create a broader hierarchy. Select important source-grounded transitions and figures for a few deliberately larger portrait/text groups; do not distribute emphasis evenly or add decoration merely to fill space.
3. **The opening remains more tapered and the descents more uniformly spaced.** The reference spreads earlier into distinct territorial groups, while v21 retains a longer triangular opening and several extended, similarly paced streams. A future composition pass should test a wider early fan-out and clearer local grouping around the existing relationships, preserving the successful tracing clearances.

V21 resolves the principal ownership defects of v20. Further work should develop editorial hierarchy and spatial grouping rather than continue moving otherwise well-associated labels.

## Final-candidate glance

After the main review, I inspected `images/landmarks-final-aurelian-families.png` at page scale and through browser detail screenshots at 1200 px page width. The tighter icon–caption gaps visibly strengthen the realm units, especially Corven, Daleshire, and Falken. This partially addresses the first remaining distinction above. The corrected ownership remains clear, and I see no new collision in those details. Everen's realm caption and house capsule still sit close together as competing headings. The expanded provenance footer remains within its allotted bottom space.

The final candidate retains the recommendation to replace v19. Its tighter landmark grouping is an improvement; it does not establish parity with the reference's broader hierarchy and composition.
