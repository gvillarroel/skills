# Editorial poster composition

Use this reference to match the visual family, not just its palette. The first gallery was rejected by the user despite passing geometry and an overly permissive aggregate rubric. Its repeated equal-width chains, empty space, small title, and sparse annotations were visible defects.

## Build the hierarchy in four scales

1. **Page:** a 2:3 silhouette for dense material, approximately 4–5% height for a strong condensed title, a continuous ivory field, narrow perimeter, and compact provenance. Avoid a large app-like toolbar or explanatory panel inside the printable field.
2. **Families:** allocate width to actual branch complexity. Origins may be central, but subsequent branches should follow their own history. Put white outlined family labels near the relevant branch, not in a rigid legend row divorced from the graph. Schematic positions may be uneven; numeric time positions may not.
3. **Entities:** use several purposeful treatments. Colored cards mark principal actors; smaller unboxed names supply supporting lineage; white image/emblem panels distinguish selected landmarks; pills identify families or movements. A card's dimensions should follow its label and importance rather than one universal box size.
4. **Detail:** subordinate dates, explanatory fragments, image credits, uncertain links, and fine context reward close reading. At whole-page scale these create texture; at 100% they must remain legible and correct.

## Distinguish the three morphologies

**Genealogy:** irregular generational intervals in a schematic composition; marriages interrupt descent spines; cadet lines end or rejoin other houses; occasional long cross-house connections use open corridors. Plain spouses and intermediate ancestors coexist with colored rulers. Selective portraits, heraldry, and serif place labels establish a second visual layer. Do not invent relatives just to create this texture. Example pattern: `usefulcharts-dynastic-genealogy`.

The founding fan is only the beginning. Compare the middle and lower thirds separately: repeated long-lived lanes can remain even after small local forks are added. Reclaim space after substantial endings and pack around consequential unions. Read [cohort composition](cohort-composition.md) for an automated starting point, then author the difficult regions. Define whether dates mean life or reign; synchronized intervals repeated across unrelated people create artificial texture. Synthetic records should also be causally coherent before layout.

**Institutional lineage:** a compact common-origin story may occupy the upper center, with small explanatory insets in adjacent free space. Branches differ in breadth, depth, and number of descendants. Allocate unequal page regions to different historical stages: a broad early family may give rise to a later family farther down, while a smaller neighboring tradition persists beside both. Derive these stages from the supplied edges. Some branches terminate; others split or receive influence. Large family headings and selected emblems establish landmarks among many smaller names. Avoid five equally populated columns or a branching diagram that becomes only independent straight chains. Pattern: `usefulcharts-branching-lineage`.

**Parallel history:** ribbons change width and position when the supplied layout emphasizes a polity or phase. Use explicit gaps and actual start/end years. Rotated compact labels can live inside longer ribbons; horizontal events sit beside them. Era dividers, a shared ruler, small illustrations, and restrained map texture make the page readable at multiple scales. Draw a continuation only when it is explicitly supplied. Avoid uninterrupted equal-width columns with an event copied at every identical interval. Pattern: `usefulcharts-parallel-history`.

Treat succession, division, and political union as different source relationships. The number of simultaneous ribbons should follow the supplied history. A narrow split bridge should join the correct ports once; a filled diagonal plus a separate orthogonal elbow looks like two competing connections. Pair a short event headline with a subordinate consequence instead of giving every annotation the same bold weight.

## Color and artwork

Use clear coral, sky blue, gold, sage, lilac, orange, and pink family colors, with a neutral precursor color when meaningful. Carry the same category through nodes and connectors. Black or near-black text usually fits these light fills; measure contrast. Keep color area varied: a mixture of colored cards and unboxed names reads differently from an all-box diagram.

The renderer includes original vector emblems, a public-domain Natural Earth locator map, and small public-domain museum portrait/object collections. Map category assignments must be supplied; the renderer does not assert geographical prevalence. Museum samples are decorative resources for synthetic demonstrations. For a factual genealogy, use correctly identified likenesses with verified provenance; never attach a convenient historical face to the wrong real person. For factual timelines, match an object's actual date and provenance rather than implying it belongs to an unrelated event.

Keep raster illustrations selective and embed them in the SVG. An illustrated accent may improve visual hierarchy; it cannot repair empty or repetitive topology. Original vector emblems are available when pictorial imagery is inappropriate.

Check the actual image background before placement. A painted checkerboard is not transparency, and a uniform gray photo rectangle may look pasted onto the paper. Prefer a source vector or an already isolated image when appropriate. Inspect the entire illustration at its placed size, including padding and its proximity to timeline bridges. Small source drawings can be more useful than repeated generic category emblems.

## Critique loop

Render the page and compare against the closest official product preview, normalized to the same width. Write the three most visible differences before editing. First repair composition and relative density, then hierarchy and connector rhythm, then typography and imagery. Inspect full resolution after every structural change. Check the revised result, not an earlier screenshot.

Use the reference as a visual target while maintaining original content and a clear author identity. Brand marks, exact text, and copied product artwork are not needed to reproduce the composition grammar. Keep copyrighted reference screenshots in local evaluation artifacts rather than publishing them in the gallery.
