# Information and editorial planning

Use this reference before layout when the input is a research pool, the intended explanation is unclear, or important information is incomplete. Keep the decisions in concise working notes or an existing brief; produce a separate planning artifact only when useful or requested.

## Test sufficiency against the explanation

State the intended reading outcome: for example, trace a succession, understand a merger, compare concurrent periods, or find a person's connection to an ancestor. Establish audience, temporal and geographic scope, and intended viewing size from the request or reasonable context.

Check both explanatory sufficiency and the [reference-level knowledge-density target](knowledge-density.md). A handful of complete records can explain a small story but still fail the requested poster density. Preserve enough breadth, typed relationships, dates and relevant context in the selection to meet the reference; do not automatically narrow the displayed story until only a sparse outline remains.

Check the minimum evidence for that outcome:

| Visual claim | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| A named entity | A stable identity and a usable label; resolve ambiguous duplicate names. |
| Descent, succession, influence, union or division | Identifiable endpoints and the actual relationship type. Dates alone do not establish a link. |
| Numeric time position or duration | Supported dates, bounds and uncertainty; preserve exact gaps. An undated entity cannot be assigned an invented coordinate on the time scale. |
| A family or category color | A defined grouping that the source supports; importance belongs in emphasis, not a fabricated category. |
| A historical explanation or illustration | Support for the contextual claim and a defensible association between the pictured subject and the record. |

An unknown birth year does not prevent a genealogy whose parentage is known; preserve the unknown and use generations. Missing relationship identities can prevent the requested ancestry claim. Distinguish those cases. Resolve conflicting evidence or mark the dispute visibly; do not silently choose the version that lays out more neatly. Keep provenance sufficient to trace displayed claims to the supplied material or cited sources.

Validate the source's actual subject and edition after fetching it. A successful
download or PDF signature does not prove that an old document link still serves
the document; inspect its title, representative content and relevant pages. For
a dated overview, separate completed events from the publication's future plans.
Corroborate consequential date conflicts or choose a justified visible precision
that does not pretend to settle them. Distinguish a multi-vehicle campaign from
its component craft before counting records or drawing operating intervals.

For a material gap, research available authorized sources or ask the smallest question that would resolve it. Continue source organization and other independent work while waiting. A provisional inventory or a narrower chart can still be useful if its limitations are explicit and it does not replace the requested outcome without agreement. For synthetic demonstrations, define a coherent fictional history before geometry and label it as such.

## Choose the displayed story

First distinguish a complete display brief from a pool of potentially relevant information. Treat supplied chart records and all explicit must-include facts as required unless the user authorizes selection. With a broader research pool, retain the original and make a traceable display selection:

- **Core:** facts that answer the reading question, including intermediate records needed to explain the actual relationships.
- **Context:** a small amount of evidence that explains a consequential change, uncertainty or contrast. Give it subordinate type or a clearly owned inset.
- **Outside this display:** irrelevant branches, repetitive background or detail beyond the stated scope. Record the reason and retain the source identity; do not delete the original evidence.

For each proposed contextual detail, explain what it helps the reader understand. Merely concerning the same institution or person does not make a detail useful to this poster. Keep the selected relationship inventory explicit enough to preserve intermediate branches and every merger input.

Do not turn `A → B → C` into an apparent direct `A → C` simply to save space. Keep B, or use a clearly labeled aggregation when the task permits it. Preserve selected records' dates, meanings and required wording. A good selection reduces distraction without changing the historical claim or stripping out the knowledge needed for the density target. For a dense-poster request, seek additional relevant support or record the content shortfall; a smaller page alone cannot turn a few claims into reference-level knowledge. Use a narrower explanation when that scope is requested, and never invent filler.

## Map meaning to the page

Choose the construction route after selecting the story. State what horizontal and vertical positions mean; reserve numeric spacing for data that supports it. Allocate width by concurrent branches, descendants, notes and complete caption footprints. Let a terminated branch release space.

Use stable colors for named families or categories; use size and treatment for importance. Distinguish relation types through connector paths and the key. Select illustrations for explanatory value, and place them with the record they explain. Preserve readable names, dates and notes before pursuing a denser silhouette.

## Critique the explanation as well as the image

After rendering, check that a reader can answer the original question and follow the decisive relationships without false shortcuts. Compare whole-page organization and dense details with the appropriate reference family. Record an observed weakness, its proposed repair, and what changed after rerendering; use [evaluation and repair](evaluation.md) for the full review.

Reopen content selection only within the authorized scope. Repair layout when required facts are crowded; do not remove those facts to improve an aesthetic score. Keep unresolved factual gaps, technical defects and visual differences separate so success in one area cannot conceal failure in another.

For a planning-only deliverable, describe the future review as an actionable sequence: render and audit the chosen records; inspect the full page and the decisive junction or dense detail; compare with the relevant reference at matched scale; record the most visible differences; repair, rerender and inspect the new revision. State which subject-specific facts and visual weaknesses the reviewer should check. The plan does not claim that any of those visual checks have already occurred. Read the acceptance criteria in [evaluation and repair](evaluation.md) when deciding what would count as finished.
